The Book of Chance

Further Reading

Where the principal claims are discussed. Reading I introduces equal dignity and practical opportunity (see Dignity and The Capability Approach below). Reading III distinguishes moral assessment from the harm that occurred (see Moral Luck and Moral Responsibility). Reading IV contrasts determinism, ontological indeterminacy, fatalism and different theories of free will (see Causal Determinism, Chance versus Randomness, Philosophical Issues in Quantum Theory, Free Will and Compatibilism). Reading X considers moral standing beyond humanity (see Environmental Ethics). These references explain the debates; none certifies Confluent Humanism as a scientific discovery.

The ideas in this book meet questions that have been debated long before the name Confluent Humanism was proposed. The values of compassion, reflection, interdependence, freedom and responsibility appear in diverse religious, philosophical and community traditions, often with important disagreements about their foundations. This book does not claim to have originated those insights or to speak on behalf of any tradition. Its proposed contribution lies in bringing a conviction about chance together with situated agency and responsibility to others.

The following open-access reference essays offer different arguments and introductions to some of these debates. They are starting points, not authorities whose existence proves the position taken here. This selection is not a comprehensive comparative account and should not be mistaken for specialist cultural review.

  • Chance versus Randomness, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chance-randomness/ — for the differences among objective chance, randomness and unpredictability.
  • Causal Determinism, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/determinism-causal/ — for the distinctions among laws, predictability and fatalism.
  • Philosophical Issues in Quantum Theory, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qt-issues/ — for the interpretation disputes behind probabilistic descriptions in physics.
  • Free Will, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/freewill/ — for competing accounts of deliberation, control and choice.
  • Incompatibilist (Nondeterministic) Theories of Free Will, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/incompatibilism-theories/ — for why indeterminacy alone does not supply a complete account of freedom.
  • Compatibilism, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/compatibilism/ — for the argument that meaningful free will may be compatible with determinism.
  • Moral Luck, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-luck/ — for questions about responsibility, circumstances and the consequences of action.
  • Moral Responsibility, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/ — for distinctions among causing harm, blameworthiness and the obligations that follow.
  • Environmental Ethics, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-environmental/ — for competing accounts of non-human moral standing and intrinsic value.
  • The Capability Approach, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/ — for Amartya Sen's and Martha Nussbaum's influential work on what people are substantively able to do or be, and why resources alone may not secure real opportunity.
  • Dignity, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dignity/ — for competing interpretations and philosophical justifications of equal human worth.
  • Feminist Ethics, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminism-ethics/ — for traditions of care, relationships and the distribution of moral responsibilities.

For longer arguments from different traditions, these published books provide further points of comparison (access may require a library or purchase):

  • Bernard Williams, Moral Luck: Philosophical Papers 1973–1980 (Cambridge University Press, 1981): https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/moral-luck/84945A1EB61E73D5676E22B6F520B3C0 — on responsibility, integrity and the effects of contingency.
  • Virginia Held, The Ethics of Care: Personal, Political, and Global (Oxford University Press, 2006): https://academic.oup.com/book/2881 — on obligations arising in relationships and institutions.
  • Martha C. Nussbaum, Creating Capabilities: The Human Development Approach (Harvard University Press, 2011): https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.4159/harvard.9780674061200/html — on dignity, freedom and substantive human opportunities.

These resources provide context and further arguments; they do not amount to peer review of this book. The work also needs informed feedback on how it represents other traditions and knowledge associated with particular places; no simulated reader perspective can provide that authorisation or expertise. Readers are encouraged to follow the disagreements as well as the agreements.
