The Book of Chance

Ways of Reflecting

The questions in this book are invitations, not assessments. They can be considered privately, shared with someone trusted or left unanswered. No personal question needs to be answered in order to read, belong or take part in a discussion. Readers dealing with grief or distress may skip a section without explanation. No record or public account is required.

Reading in two ways. The twelve readings may be read consecutively, passing over the questions, or explored one at a time with others. Neither approach is more faithful to the book. In a group, people may discuss an idea without describing their private lives.

The Reflection of Chance. At a suitable moment, consider five areas without requiring yourself to write about each one:

  1. Circumstances. What shaped this day or week beyond my control?
  2. Choices. What did I do, and what influenced my decisions?
  3. Uncertainty. What alternatives can I imagine without pretending to know what would have happened?
  4. Learning and appreciation. What did I understand, notice or value?
  5. Possibility. What might I prepare for, change, accept or ask help with next?

Some people find a short regular entry useful because changes become visible over time. Others prefer an occasional conversation, a sketch or quiet thought. A person may skip any question, stop when reflection becomes unhelpful or decide that rest is the wiser choice.

The Act of Possibility. When reasonably able, consider a welcome, bounded action that may improve another person's opportunity or the conditions of the living world. It might be an explanation, an introduction made with consent, a shared resource, a practical contribution or participation in a wider project. There is no quota, required proof or guarantee of return.

The Turning of Chance. At the New Year or another personally meaningful time, some people may choose to look back on the year, recognise support received, remember losses, consider what they learned and identify what might matter next. A year need not have been successful to deserve honest reflection, and its hardships need not become a celebratory lesson.

Reflection with others. If discussing the readings in a group, make room for people to disagree or remain quiet. Invite participants to speak from their own beliefs rather than on behalf of a faith, culture or community. Ask what a story illuminates and what it leaves out. Protect disclosures and allow people to pass on any question. A good discussion may end with different interpretations and greater understanding. Any detailed guidance for facilitators belongs in separate resources, not in a requirement imposed by this book.
