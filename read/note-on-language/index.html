The Book of Chance

A Note on Language

Chance. A word with several uses in this book. Fundamental chance means ontological indeterminacy: a proposed feature of reality in which at least some later events are not fixed by the complete past and natural laws. Uncertainty concerns what we do not know or cannot predict, which can exist even if events are determined. Circumstantial luck concerns advantages, hardships and events we did not choose or fully control. Practical prospects are the options, barriers and risks that learning, preparation, action and institutions can sometimes change. A wider set of practical options does not necessarily prove a numerically higher probability of one outcome. None of these meanings proves the others.

Confluence. The meeting of streams; here, the convergence of events, circumstances, causes and choices through which lives unfold and influence one another.

Confluent Humanism. The developing philosophical worldview presented in this book. The name does not imply recognition as an established academic school.

Fate. The course that comes to pass, not a prewritten destiny. Someone may use the word affectionately or poetically without claiming that events were arranged for them.

Agency. In this book, the capacity to deliberate, choose meaningfully and respond to reasons within the conditions of a life. People's effective freedom, or practical room to act, varies with resources, coercion, safety and other constraints. A person's ability to weigh and act on reasons matters in either a deterministic or indeterministic picture, though philosophers disagree about what more freedom requires. Randomness by itself does not establish freedom.

Determinism and fatalism. Determinism holds, broadly, that states of the world together with governing laws fix later states. Fatalism holds that certain outcomes will occur regardless of what people do. They are distinct: under determinism, decisions and actions may be among the causes through which outcomes happen.

Moral luck. The problem of judging people when their circumstances, character or actions' outcomes depend partly on factors outside their control. The book distinguishes appraisal of an act from duties to address harm that actually occurs.

RNG. An informal borrowing from the language of random-number generation. Here it is a metaphor for chance, not a claim that the universe operates as a literal computer program. To improve one's RNG means to improve prospects through conditions and preparation, not to accumulate luck.

Blessing of chance. A poetic expression of gratitude or wonder at good fortune, without implying that chance intentionally favoured someone.

The Reflection of Chance. A voluntary practice of considering circumstances, choices, learning, appreciation and future possibilities, privately by default.

The Act of Possibility. A voluntary, reasonable action that may widen another person's opportunities or benefit the living world, without requiring reward, reciprocity or proof of impact.

The Turning of Chance. A proposed annual occasion for personal and communal reflection, not a compulsory observance.
