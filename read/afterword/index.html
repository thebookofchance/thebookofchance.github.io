The Book of Chance

Afterword — What We Can Widen

Imagine the child from the beginning of this book grown older, standing at a door they have not opened before. There are reasons to enter and reasons to wait. Some are known; others will become visible only much later. The person brings a history they did not choose in full, and a capacity—however constrained—to respond to what is before them.

They might enter. They might turn away. They might ask someone to come with them. The choice will join a history already in motion.

This book has offered a conviction: that the course of the world is not entirely contained in its beginning, and that genuine chance belongs to reality. A careful reader may reject it and still recognise the unfinished work before this person at the door. The conviction informs the author's understanding of possibility; it cannot, by itself, establish what makes the next action fair.

The ethical invitation is more immediate. People inherit uneven chances. They can sometimes change their own circumstances, and sometimes change the conditions another person inherits. That power does not make them masters of every outcome. It gives them a reason to notice whom their choices touch.

A door can be opened carelessly, without asking who stands on the other side. It can be held open with expectations attached. It can be closed in the name of protecting those already inside. And sometimes the most useful action is to repair the entrance so that no one needs a special invitation.

There will be days when we are the person opening the door. There will be days when we need someone to open it for us. Neither position defines our worth.

We do not need to know how every story ends before we treat the next person with care. We may never know what came of an introduction, an apology, a refusal to exploit, a shared lesson or a welcome at the right moment.

What follows belongs to more lives than our own.

— H
