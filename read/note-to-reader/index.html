The Book of Chance

A Note to the Reader

Every life begins in the middle of other lives. We arrive among circumstances that were not waiting for our permission: a family history, a particular place, the care or neglect of institutions, a language already spoken, a world already changing. Later we make choices that matter, but we never make them from nowhere.

This book offers a philosophy called Confluent Humanism, from confluence: the meeting of streams. It holds, as a revisable philosophical conviction, that chance is fundamental to reality and the future is not wholly predetermined. Causes, circumstances and meaningful choices still shape what unfolds. The reasons for this conviction, and its limitations, are examined in Reading IV; it is not presented as a scientific finding.

Not knowing what will happen is different from living in a world where some events are not fixed by the past. Both differ from inheriting advantages or hardships, and from changing the practical options open to someone. A safer crossing can reduce hazards even when we cannot assign a precise probability of harm to any one traveller; it certainly cannot establish that nature is indeterminate. The distinctions matter because the actions we recommend must make sense even when our metaphysical beliefs are disputed.

Five values guide the life proposed here: Hope, Acceptance, Reflection, Growth and Community. They emerge from a commitment to dignity, careful attention, autonomy and life shared with others; they cannot be deduced from randomness. You need not share the author's view of nature to consider whether those values deserve a place in your own life.

Readers of religious faith, readers without one, and those still deciding what they believe are invited to disagree. Reading this book requires no membership, donation or disclosure of private experience.

The question beneath the book is simple enough to carry into ordinary life: knowing that so much of our story was shaped beyond our choosing, how shall we act toward those whose stories our choices will touch?
