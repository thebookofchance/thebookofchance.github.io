# Public Terminology Standard v1.0

**22 November 2026 · Editorial direction from H**

## Purpose

The public literature, reader resources and website of Confluent Humanism and the Fellowship of Chance use **chance, uncertainty, circumstances, prospects and possibility** precisely and naturally, without gaming-specific jargon. This is an editorial vocabulary decision, not a new philosophical doctrine.

## Core distinctions

| Intended meaning | Preferred language |
| --- | --- |
| Fundamental indeterminacy held as a philosophical conviction | **Chance**, with the claim stated explicitly where relevant |
| What cannot be known or reliably predicted | **Uncertainty** |
| Advantages, harms or starting conditions not chosen | **Circumstances**, **fortune** or **circumstantial luck** |
| Conditions that may make opportunities more accessible | **Improving practical prospects**, **widening possibilities** |
| Voluntary generosity supported by favourable circumstances | **Sharing good fortune**, **creating possibility** |

These ideas are related, but should not be treated as interchangeable. Increased access to opportunity is not proof of a specific probability or guaranteed outcome. Randomness alone does not demonstrate agency or supply an ethical obligation.

## Style rules

- Explain unfamiliar philosophical terms in ordinary language before using specialist terminology.
- Do not import vocabulary or metaphors from games, algorithms or computer-generated luck into the public philosophy.
- Avoid claims that prayer, donations or positive thinking can control chance.
- Avoid implying that effort alone can overcome structural barriers.
- Preserve the names **Confluent Humanism**, **Fellowship of Chance** and **The Book of Chance**.
- Record substantive revisions in an editorial change log and keep archival editions for traceability.

## Scope

Applies to public website pages, online readers, current digital editions, new reader resources, video scripts, social captions and future print files. Original archived planning documents are preserved as historical records rather than silently rewritten.
