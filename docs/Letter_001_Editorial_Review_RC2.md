## The central argument

The lives of people we may never meet are affected by the work, infrastructure, institutional choices and care we inherit and leave in place. We need not know every consequence or demand gratitude to take their lives seriously. Responsibility must be proportionate to knowledge and power, attentive to the affected people, and open to correction.

## Revisions from RC1 to RC2

1. **Made the opening explicitly illustrative.** “Imagine a handrail” cannot be mistaken for a sourced report about a particular building.
2. **Deepened the distinction between individual generosity and institutions.** Dependable public systems, safe work and accountability matter even when nobody acts from personal kindness.
3. **Retitled an internal movement** from “A letter that arrives too late” to “A message left behind” to describe its fictional found-note scene more precisely.
4. **Strengthened the case for meaningful consultation.** Listening can change a solution, not merely endorse a decision already made.
5. **Clarified stewardship toward future generations.** The aim is to preserve options and capacity for correction rather than dictate others’ futures.
6. **Strengthened the conclusion about criticism and feedback.** Responsibility does not require omniscience, but does require learning when effects are revealed.
7. **Updated the author’s note** to distinguish Issue 001’s inquiry into unseen interdependence from the planned Issue 003’s broader question about competing obligations to strangers.

## Relationship to the book

Builds explicitly on *The Book of Chance*, especially Reading VIII (*Shared Fortune*), Reading X (*The Living World*) and Reading XI (*Stewardship*). It remains independent of any Fellowship membership requirement. It does **not** assert that random processes prove moral obligations, guarantee beneficial outcomes or excuse institutional harms.

## Integrity and ethical checks

- Handrail and found-note scenes are declared illustrative; no interviews, case histories or real testimonials are claimed.
- No empirical numbers, direct quotations or purported research findings need independent statistical verification.
- Concern for strangers is balanced by limits on time, capacity, foreseeable effects and power.
- Disability/accessibility appears as a reason for shared infrastructure, not a pretext to pity an unknown individual.
- Ethics are offered as revisable arguments rather than supernatural rules or proprietary revelations.
- Reflection questions and optional practice demand no disclosure, donation, member registration or mandatory service.
- The text does not imply an active Fellowship charity or support programme.
