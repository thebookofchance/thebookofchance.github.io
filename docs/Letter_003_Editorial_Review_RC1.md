## Purpose and central argument

This completes the first planned three-Letter group. Its question is **normative and bounded**: what respect and assistance may reasonably be owed to unfamiliar people, especially when personal commitments, capacity, uncertainty, and institutions are involved?

- Letter 001 traces how unseen people and institutions shape lives. Letter 003 considers which duties those connections can generate.
- Letter 002 distinguishes decision quality from outcome quality; Letter 003 focuses on competing claims and the limits of required sacrifice.
- Thesis: strangers retain moral dignity regardless of acquaintance; practical duties differ with vulnerability, knowledge, capability, promises, consent, and public responsibility. The result is not a formula that judges human worth or requires endless giving.

## Editorial architecture

1. An imagined supermarket encounter introduces the obligation/limits dilemma.
2. **A claim before an introduction** — respect and duties not to exploit, even without personal ties.
3. **The people we have promised to** — genuine special obligations need not imply unequal dignity.
4. **What a need may ask of us** — serious need, means, consent, and moral demandingness.
5. **Help without possession** — power, privacy, refusal and dignity.
6. **When a responsibility belongs to more than one person** — charity and just institutions are not interchangeable.
7. **The reach of an ordinary life** — considered closing and two private reflection questions plus optional action.
8. **A note on this letter** — source relation, fictional disclosure, intellectual debts and invitation to disagree.

## Philosophical sources and distinctness

The letter uses ideas debated in moral philosophy without asserting that they originated in Confluent Humanism. Verified reference targets include:

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, [Special Obligations](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/special-obligations/) — special relationships, friendship, promises, impartial regard.
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, [The Principle of Beneficence in Applied Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/principle-beneficence/) — when helping may be obligatory, and proportional cost/risk.
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, [Moral Demands and Permissions/Prerogatives](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-demands-permissions/) — debate on duties exceeding comfortable sacrifice.

This philosophical context is complementary, not a claim that all cited views endorse the letter's synthesis. The canonical foundation is *The Book of Chance*, particularly **Shared Fortune**, **Fellowship** and **Stewardship**.

## Ethics and publication boundaries

- Supermarket/directions episodes are invented scenarios, not reported cases or personal testimonials.
- No one is instructed to donate, expose another person's hardship, join the Fellowship, or sacrifice essential income.
- Existing commitments to dependants and capacity limits are treated seriously, while exclusion and exploitation remain unacceptable.
- Public accountability is advocated as a future standard, not asserted to be operational at the Fellowship.
- Rights/permission review for any commercial physical edition remains a distinct task.
