# The Book of Chance — Publications Standard
## Editorial, visual, production and release rules for the H catalogue

**Edition:** 1.0-RC1 · 22 November 2026  
**Status:** Proposed house standard, for founder/author approval. It records rules actually demonstrated by *The Book of Chance* and recommended extensions for future titles; it is not proof that any outside review or legal registration has occurred.  
**Primary reference:** *The Book of Chance: On the Lives We Inherit and the Futures We Shape*, illustrated manuscript v0.12 and its approved Light/Dark cover editions.  
**Supporting references:** Foundation Identity v1.0-RC1; Identity & Values Charter RC1; Journal Series Editorial & Print Plan v0.9.

---

## 1. What this standard is for

Create a recognisable, durable body of literature without forcing all texts into the same length, cover or voice. Every publication must offer a distinct reason to exist. *The Book of Chance* remains the foundational book; later writing may expand, challenge, illustrate or apply its ideas, but should not quietly rewrite its settled text.

**Public access principle:** Core writings, essays, discussion resources and digital reflection materials are freely readable without registration, donation or membership. A future physical edition may be priced to recover costs or support continued publishing, but must not be falsely represented as an open order or a tax-deductible donation. Digital access must remain possible without buying a copy.

**Authority principle:** H is a pseudonymous authorial identity, not a prophet or authority exempt from criticism. A new Letter is an interpretation or argument, not an additional foundational commandment. The philosophy invites religious, spiritual and secular readers to agree or disagree.

## 2. What is inherited directly from the foundational book

The book's identifiable publishing DNA is:

- **Title and attribution:** a measured serif-led title and an isolated **H** byline; the exact book title is *The Book of Chance*, with subtitle *On the Lives We Inherit and the Futures We Shape*.
- **Visual language:** fine intersecting, continuing paths, asymmetry with balance; charcoal, quiet neutrals, white and restrained blue. Existing approved cover artwork is the reference; do not redesign it or replace the accepted website emblem when issuing a new text.
- **Editorial rhythm:** human situation → philosophical claim → complications and limits → consequences for living → optional reflection. The 12 foundational readings frequently use parables, but not every short essay must.
- **Argument discipline:** philosophical belief in fundamental chance and an open future is presented as a *revisable conviction*, not established empirical science; meaningful agency is not inferred from randomness alone. Ethics must be argued from dignity, care and responsibility, not deduced from chance.
- **Five values:** Hope, Acceptance, Reflection, Growth, Community. Never turn them into a hierarchy, compulsory points system or claim that readers can earn luck.
- **Respectful limits:** avoid blaming people for illness, hardship or failure; avoid promises of rescue, inevitability, supernatural favour, or moral worth earned by giving.
- **Practices:** Reflection of Chance and Act of Possibility are voluntary, private and open to adaptation. Consent, boundaries and material limits matter.
- **Institutional truthfulness:** no publication may imply that an unlaunched Council, local fellowship, mentoring programme, relief fund, donation system or charity campaign already operates.

The manifesto/charter is a **working organisational document**, not a substitute for the independently readable book. If the philosophy and organisation evolve, distinguish that development from a textual correction to the founding book.

## 3. A shelf with distinct editorial purposes

| Collection | Role | Typical length (guideline) | Reader expectation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Foundational Writings** | Longer arguments explaining the philosophical framework | Varies; full books can exceed 20,000 words | Careful reasoning, doubts, sources and independent readership |
| **Letters from H** | One focused question and a developed perspective | 1,400–2,400 words | A complete, approachable essay; no status of revelation |
| **The Practice Collection** | Journals, worksheets and practical exercises | As needed for usability | Clear instructions, useful writing space, reader ownership and privacy |
| **Stories and Parables** | Imagined experiences illuminating difficult ideas | 500–4,000 words/story | Fiction labelled as fiction; ambiguity, not sermon illustrations pretending to be facts |
| **Fellowship & Community** | Discussion guides, rules, conversations and public accountabilities | Session-based | Facilitator prompts, consent/safeguards, openness to disagreement |
| **Selected Essays & Booklets** | An accessible conceptual overview or deeper thematic argument | 3,000–12,000 words | An original contribution, not a stretched summary |

Avoid issuing several products with materially identical interior content merely to expand the number of downloads. Light/Dark cover choices are permitted where the user-facing tradition warrants them; content equality must be verified and clearly stated.

## 4. The editorial voice of H

**Tone:** quiet confidence, humane specificity, warmth without flattery, room for uncertainty and dissent. The author may pose questions; it should not claim to read the reader's mind, demand loyalty or speak with prophetic certainty.

**Language:** New Zealand/Commonwealth English (`organisation`, `favour`, `recognise`, `practise` as verb), accessible sentences with varied rhythm, spare metaphor, no motivational slogans in place of an argument. Prefer an observed detail or a careful imagined scene to abstraction. Avoid repeating “chance” and “possibility” as empty ornamental words.

**Originality:** Develop new lines of inquiry; do not recycle entire passages from the book or another Letter. Reference the relevant reading when extending a theme. Explicitly acknowledge intellectual relatives when relying on a distinctive external theory. No fabricated quotations, verified-sounding anecdotes, statistics, testimonials or citations.

**Fairness:** Include the strongest plausible objection to the central position when useful. Do not turn responsibility into an instruction that vulnerable readers must give more. Avoid compulsory confession, guilt, fear, exclusivity and manipulative donation framing.

**Scenes/parables:** Identify invented illustrations in notes when they might otherwise appear factual. No reader testimonial is authored without consent and a documented, accurate source.

**Discourse:** State that interpretation and critique are welcome. A philosophical text can ask readers to reconsider their assumptions without requiring them to join an organisation.

## 5. Standard architecture by format

### 5A. Letters from H — the primary recurring form

1. **Series + issue number:** `Letters from H · No. 01`.
2. **Specific title:** one question or theme, not a generic slogan.
3. **Opening image, scene or dilemma:** within the first two paragraphs, with no false factual claim.
4. **Argument:** 4–7 titled movements, preferably one coherent thesis with a meaningful limitation/counterpoint.
5. **Resonant closing:** no promise of transformation or recruitment demand.
6. **For reflection:** typically two optional questions and one modest optional practice.
7. **A note on this letter:** links to its foundations, editorial distinction, any illustrative fiction, and specific further-reading sources when needed.
8. **End matter:** author byline, issue date, edition marker and free-use/access statement.

**Website:** full HTML article with a stable URL. **Download:** a clean, printable PDF; EPUB is optional but recommended where practical. A Letter must be readable without downloading, signing up or visiting the Fellowship page.

### 5B. Booklets, guides and conversations

- Open with a purpose and intended audience.
- Establish where the material builds upon the foundational book and where it contributes new teaching, interpretation or practical structure.
- Make exercises voluntary; avoid collecting personal responses by default.
- For group activities: note duration, facilitator responsibility, consent, accessibility, disagreement and safeguarding boundaries.
- If a source or quoted tradition is central, include a properly checked reference note.

### 5C. Journals

- One clear purpose per journal; each section should be usable without an induction session.
- Provide editable PDF fields where compatible, and a print-usable alternative when possible.
- No streaks, metrics or sharing obligations. Explain that the writing stays on the reader's device and that PDF readers differ in how they save fields.
- The digital 6×9-inch layout can inform a future physical edition, but a print proof is not a final printer-ready master.

## 6. Visual system

### Colour and marks

| Token | Working value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Dark charcoal | `#26313B` | Body text, lines and cover type |
| Ink alternative | `#172633` | Fine motifs and strong contrast |
| Accent blue | `#286CB8` | Small highlights, issue numbers, links |
| Paper | `#FAFAF7` | Interior and web background |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Cover and figure breathing space |
| Secondary grey | `#68747C` | Metadata/captions where accessible |

The palette is derived from website production assets and harmonised to the existing book covers; it is **not a formal measurement of every pixel in the cover artwork**. Text and background contrast must be checked in each export.

### Typography and hierarchy

- **Display:** elegant, readable serif; mimic the book's restrained high-contrast literary setting. Use an available legally distributable serif as the PDF font; do not bundle proprietary font files.
- **Body:** readable serif for essays, with generous line height and short-to-medium measure; sans serif for metadata, tabs and navigational instructions.
- **Cover order:** series mark → title → quiet motif → subtitle or issue line → H byline.
- **Interior:** front matter with edition identity; running headers/pagination as appropriate; clearly differentiated headings; deliberate paragraph spacing; restrained blue rule/motif rather than clip-art icons.
- **Artwork:** paths may cross, branch, diverge or continue. Their function must relate to a text, not merely fill space. Do not repurpose the official fellowship logo as a universal decorative stamp.

**Print:** use 6×9 in as an initial editorial-prototype trim where appropriate, but confirm platform-specific margins, bleed, spine, page count, paper and print specifications before sale. **Digital PDFs:** embed fonts where permitted, provide selectable text, meaningful document metadata and real links, with accessible tagging evaluated separately.

**Avoid fake publication details.** Do not invent an ISBN, publisher address, legal status, copyright registration or established imprint. `The Book of Chance · H` may be used as a catalogue identity without presenting it as a registered company.

## 7. Identifiers, versions and metadata

**Canonical identifier:** `BOC-<series>-<three-digit-number>`: e.g. `BOC-LFH-001`. Book materials may retain their original release identifiers. Each catalogue entry records:

- unique ID, canonical title, subtitle (if any), author `H`, series and number;
- initial publication date and last revision date (ISO 8601);
- current public edition (`1.0` for first approved issue), status (`draft`, `review candidate`, `released`, `revised`, `withdrawn`);
- short description, tags, relationships to book readings, and link destinations;
- file format(s), language (`en-NZ`), rights/permissions statement and a factual sources register if needed;
- an editorial change note distinguishing substantive revision from typography/metadata correction.

A **draft is not automatically a released work**. The production files may be staged for publishing before the author approves them. No silent replacement of substantially changed philosophical passages; publish an edition/revision note.

## 8. Rights, privacy and financial boundaries

- Confirm that all illustrations and external quotations are licensed/owned for publication. Do not claim all material was produced solely by a human where this is not established. Editorial process disclosure wording is a separate founder/publisher policy decision and must not include false assertions.
- Do not collect private journal writing, discussion responses or reader stories as a condition for using literature. Explain voluntary forms and use separate consent for unrelated mailing lists.
- Where a future printed copy is sold, distinguish product price, printing costs and any voluntary donation. Never imply payment affects luck, dignity, member status or attention.
- Never promise that future charities, campaigns, mentorship matches or assistance programmes exist merely because they are described in literature.

## 9. Production and editorial quality gates

| Gate | Required evidence before marking PASS |
|---|---|
| 01 — Source alignment | Relevant book and charter passages checked; no contradiction concealed |
| 02 — Editorial value | Clear distinct thesis, no repetitive padding, useful objection or limit |
| 03 — Facts and sources | Specific claims checked; real quotations linked; invented scenes signalled |
| 04 — Ethics and accessibility | No coercion, stigmatization or unnecessary reader burden; usable text and contrast |
| 05 — Proofreading | Names, terminology, punctuation, issue number and links consistent |
| 06 — Design and export | HTML/PDF/EPUB as promised; legible page previews; no clipping; metadata present |
| 07 — Website integration | Stable page route, library link, search/discovery as appropriate, no dead links |
| 08 — Human review | H or delegated editor approves voice, publication status and any sensitive material |
| 09 — Live verification | Real browser/device check after deployment, download check, archival original preserved |

If gates 08–09 are not complete, the artifact must be called an **editorial candidate / deployment package**, not a fully independently verified public release.

2. `BOC-LFH-002` **When Good Choices Lead to Bad Outcomes** — next planned Letter, not written yet.
3. `BOC-LFH-003` **What Do We Owe to Strangers?** — third planned Letter, not written yet; differentiate it from Issue 001 by focusing on moral limits and competing obligations.

Then progress only after review to:

**Collection 2:** *The Five Values* (illustrated introductory booklet).  
**Collection 3:** *Conversations of Chance* (12 carefully structured discussion sessions).  
**Collection 4:** *The Little Book of Possibilities* (substantial accessible companion).  
**Collection 5:** *The Parables of Chance* (original stories, with clear fiction labels).

After these recommended publications are completed and reviewed, begin planning the Fellowship's YouTube/video channel. Video is **not** a concurrent production phase.

## 11. Open founder/publisher decisions

1. Final approval of the house standard, the inaugural Letter's wording and public release date.
2. Whether new Letters should carry individual EPUB downloads alongside PDF/HTML (Issue 001 includes EPUB as a trial format).
3. Whether to offer Light/Dark alternate covers to collections beyond journals and major books. Default: no unnecessary duplicates.
4. Final legal/rights statement and any publishing imprint registration or ISBN arrangement before commercial print.
5. Accessibility verification with assistive technology and real-device user testing.

---

*The standard is intended to protect the integrity of the literature, not to make it difficult to produce. A short, useful Letter is better than a long text that repeats a teaching the reader already understands.*
