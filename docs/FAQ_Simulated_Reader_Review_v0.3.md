## 1. Scope, method and source boundaries

This review examines every answer in the 26-question FAQ against the actual current texts in the project, principally:

- *Fellowship of Chance Foundational Identity v1.0-RC2*, particularly Sections 3–6 and 9–11;
- *Identity and Values Charter v1.0-RC2*, particularly the identity, values, membership, boundaries and current-status passages;
- *A Beginner's Guide to Confluent Humanism v0.2*;
- the published *Book of Chance* source and its *Publications Standard 1.0-RC1*;
- *Public Terminology Standard v1.0* and the present website's public-library structure.

Eight reader perspectives were simulated: (1) newcomer with no background; (2) philosophical sceptic; (3) religious reader; (4) secular reader wary of authority; (5) reader with low digital confidence; (6) prospective adult participant considering money and privacy; (7) reader concerned with hardship, disability and unequal starting conditions; and (8) editor/website accessibility reviewer. These are **analytical lenses**, not real participants, survey respondents or measured reactions. No claim is made that any reader has actually found the answers persuasive.

The review tests clarity and consistency, not the empirical truth of the metaphysical position. The RC2 foundational candidates are reference documents **under review**, not enacted policy. External legal, registration and organisational facts have not been independently verified by this report.

## 2. Executive verdict

**Result: Suitable for website staging as an explicitly labelled editorial candidate, with release gates remaining.** The FAQ makes difficult objections visible, does not claim science proves the open future, distinguishes the proposed organisation from operational services, and does not pressure readers to join, donate or agree. The current 26-question / five-theme format is useful for both linear reading and searching by topic.

Three issues mattered enough for v0.3 edits:

1. **Source and edition mislabelling:** the endnote of the v0.2 manuscript still cited RC1 and displayed v0.1, contradicting its actual revision state. Corrected to RC2 and v0.3, with their candidate status explicit.
2. **Public access versus proposed member-only resources:** current library books and journals are free and publicly accessible, while a proposed membership framework anticipates some supplementary Member resources. Q4 now discloses the unresolved distinction rather than silently implying it is settled.
3. **Trust and status nuance:** Q19, Q20, Q22 and Q24 now more clearly distinguish a newsletter/interest list from membership, disclose AI assistance, note that H-appointed oversight is not automatically independent, and acknowledge criticism of the senior-recognition financial component.

Minor edits also made the description of religious classification more direct and specified that any external-charity lottery remains an unauthorised/deferred *draw among eligible charities*, separate from needs-based aid.

## 5. Cross-source and policy tensions requiring a human decision

### A. Public supplemental resources
The working Identity/Charter RC2 describes certain supplementary books and journals as possible Member resources. The actual website and the *Publications Standard* make current digital books, essays, discussion guides and journals free without registration. The FAQ now **reports both facts**. A founder decision is still required on whether public digital access remains permanent, whether only future *additional* resources can be restricted, and how any change will be announced. **Do not use this FAQ to imply the decision has already been made.**

### B. Senior Member financial contribution
The proposed combination of sustained service and affordable regular financial giving can appear to conflict with the principle that contribution does not buy rank. The zero-affordable-contribution exception and the prohibition on donation-size weighting reduce but do not erase the legitimacy concern. The FAQ reports the current proposal, including the ability to question it; it should not be described as a ratified membership system.

### C. Founder independence
Anonymity for public authorship is compatible with transparency about actual office and money only if lawful verification, independent control, conflicts rules and an impartial complaints path exist *in practice*. The FAQ describes these as future requirements, not protections already available.

### D. Organisational status
No public website copy should advertise an existing Council, Member help service, locally recognised branches, official charity campaign or financial contribution mechanism without further evidence. A public interest form alone does not create membership.

## 6. Reading-flow and copyediting review

- The five themes are logical: basics → metaphysics/agency → values/practice → faith → institutional trust.
- The first ten questions supply a fair argumentative sequence, including genuine metaphysical disagreement.
- Repetition around future-service caveats is justified in each relevant answer because visitors may arrive from search and read a single question.
- The repeated charity/assistance distinction in Q25 and Q26 is appropriate for safeguarding because an independent reader may only open one answer.
- The ending points newcomers to the complete library without inventing paid tiers or special benefits.
- The questions are identified as editorially anticipated; no fabricated community voices or testimonials are implied.
- Preserve New Zealand spelling and the retired-term prohibition. A whole-source term scan should be included in website QA.

## 7. Design and technical reading checks

The current v0.3 artifact has a **17-page 6×9-inch PDF proof**, a cover-bearing EPUB with archive integrity checked, and structured data for 26 questions. The PDF title, author (**H**) and subject metadata are present. The web version uses native expandable questions and local-only search/filtering, with a no-results state and no account or data submission.

**These checks do not demonstrate PDF/UA tagging, full WCAG conformance, real screen-reader behaviour, accessibility in every e-reader, rights clearance, independent human comprehension, or verified live hosting.** The cover is a *proposed* design rather than an approved master. `noindex,follow` is a staging instruction, **not** access control: a deployed URL is still publicly accessible.

## 8. Release recommendation and required gates

**Recommendation:** Retain the 26-question content and its five-theme structure. Use v0.3 as an editorial candidate for website staging and reader feedback. Do not perform another large rewrite unless independent readers identify confusion or the foundational policy changes.

Before treating the FAQ as a final, indexed public release:

1. H reviews and approves the actual v0.3 answers and decides whether to accept or revise the proposed cover.
2. Conduct real newcomer, sceptical and religious-reader tests, including at least one participant who uses assistive technology where feasible.
3. Reconcile open/public digital resources against future Member-only resource policy; review Senior status funding requirements explicitly.
4. Check current website status, form language, downloadable files and every contact/assistance claim live after deployment.
5. Record a release date, copyright/permission statement as applicable, and an edition change record. Remove `noindex` only after that decision.

**Final assessment:** Editorially sound for a **review-stage web resource**, not an operational Fellowship policy or independent-release certification.
